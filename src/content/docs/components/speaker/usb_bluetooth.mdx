The `usb_bluetooth` speaker platform streams audio to a Bluetooth speaker (an A2DP sink), through a USB Bluetooth
dongle set up with the [USB Bluetooth](/components/usb_bluetooth/) component and its `a2dp_speaker` option.

Bluetooth audio is 16-bit stereo at 44.1 kHz. To play audio at another rate, put a
[Resampler Speaker](/components/speaker/resampler/) in front of this speaker.

> [!WARNING]
> Audio and voice components consume a significant amount of resources (RAM, CPU) on the device. Bluetooth
> components add to that. A board with PSRAM, such as an ESP32-P4 or ESP32-S3 with PSRAM, is recommended.

```yaml
# Example configuration entry
usb_bluetooth:
  - bluedroid: true
    a2dp_speaker: 4C:72:74:XX:XX:XX

esp32_ble:

speaker:
  - platform: usb_bluetooth
    id: bt_speaker
```

## Configuration Variables

- **buffer_duration** (*Optional*, [Time](/guides/configuration-types/#time)): How much audio the buffer between
  ESPHome and Bluetooth holds. Defaults to `500ms`.
- **usb_bluetooth_id** (*Optional*, [ID](/guides/configuration-types/#id)): The
  [USB Bluetooth](/components/usb_bluetooth/) entry to use. It must have `a2dp_speaker`. Defaults to the only one in
  YAML.
- All other options from [Speaker Component](/components/speaker/).

The `sample_rate` (`44100`), `num_channels` (`2`) and `bits_per_sample` (`16bit`) are fixed by Bluetooth audio. They
can be read by a resampler or mixer speaker that sends audio to this speaker.

## Behavior

- While the speaker is not connected, audio is dropped, so a media player does not wait for a speaker that is off.
- The stream to the speaker starts when audio comes and is suspended a few seconds after it stops.
- The volume is sent to the speaker over AVRCP (absolute volume). A speaker without absolute volume gets the samples
  scaled instead. Mute sends silence.
